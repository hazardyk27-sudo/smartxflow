# Stage 2 Focused Research Protocol

STATUS: ACTIVE
PURPOSE: Keep Stage 2 compact, reliable and model-friendly without eliminating matches just because external information is sparse.

## Core principle

Stage 2 is not a general football-news collection pass. It is a focused test of the frozen Stage 1 SXF thesis.

More information is not automatically better. Prefer a small number of high-value, market-relevant facts over broad unstructured research.

## Mandatory 3+1 structure

For every carried Stage 1 match, research only these mandatory areas first:

1. **Critical squad impact**
   - Maximum 2-3 material squad facts.
   - Focus on likely starters, role/position, replacement quality and direct relevance to the selected market.
   - Ignore low-impact absences that do not materially affect the thesis.

2. **Market-relevant recent performance**
   - Maximum 2 material findings.
   - Do not use W-D-L strings as sufficient evidence.
   - 1X2: real performance strength, opponent quality and meaningful home/away effects.
   - O/U: attacking/defensive production, tempo and goal-creation/concession profile.
   - BTTS: whether each team has a credible scoring path.

3. **Strongest counter-case**
   - Mandatory single best reason why the frozen Stage 1 thesis could be wrong.
   - Research this actively; do not merely repeat a generic caution.
   - The strongest contradiction must survive into Stage 3 as structured counterevidence.

+1. **Research coverage / source quality**
   - Record `HIGH | MEDIUM | LOW` coverage.
   - Sparse research is not negative evidence.

## Information budget

Default maximum: **6 meaningful Stage 2 facts per match**.

Recommended distribution:
- up to 2 critical squad/context facts;
- up to 2 market-relevant performance facts;
- 1 strongest supporting fact;
- 1 strongest contradicting fact.

Do not keep collecting marginal facts once the research question is answered.

## Early-stop rule

Stage 2 research may stop when all are true:
- critical squad status is sufficiently understood for the market;
- 1-2 strong market-relevant performance findings are available;
- the strongest plausible counter-case has been researched;
- source quality/coverage can be classified.

Additional articles/statistics should not be collected merely for volume.

## UNKNOWN is not CONTRADICTED

Missing or unavailable information must never be treated as negative evidence.

- `UNKNOWN` / low coverage => no Stage 2 boost, but no automatic penalty.
- Only verified adverse evidence may create a counterevidence penalty.
- A strong Stage 1 thesis remains eligible even when external coverage is weak.
- Small/obscure leagues must not be downgraded solely because reporting is sparse.

## Trigger-based deep research

Detailed tactical analysis is optional and should be triggered only when useful, such as:
- Stage 1 signal is strong but its football cause is unclear;
- unusual underdog/fake-sharp/repricing behavior needs explanation;
- recent coach/system change may be material;
- a clear style mismatch appears decisive;
- mandatory Stage 2 findings conflict with each other.

Otherwise do not expand into broad tactical research.

## H2H rule

H2H is secondary context, not a primary Stage 2 driver.

Give it meaningful weight only when the comparison is recent and the coach/squad/style context is sufficiently similar. Otherwise treat it as a light contextual fact.

## Source efficiency

Prefer one strong source over multiple weak sources.

Source tiers:
- **Tier A — Primary:** club, federation, UEFA/FIFA/competition, official squad/lineup/coach statement.
- **Tier B — High reliability:** Reuters, AP, BBC and comparable established reporting.
- **Tier C — Specialist/local:** credible team-focused reporters and established local sports media.
- **Tier D — Weak/aggregator:** unsourced aggregators, SEO summaries, rumor/social posts.

Rules:
- One Tier A/B source is normally sufficient for one factual claim.
- A critical claim supported only by Tier C/D should receive a second independent verification when feasible.
- Tier D alone must not materially change a Stage 2 verdict.

## Claim-level evidence

Do not bundle multiple claims and opposite-direction facts into one generic evidence row.

Each important claim should preserve:
- factual claim;
- source;
- observed_at when available;
- source tier;
- `SUPPORTS | CONTRADICTS | NEUTRAL` relationship to the frozen Stage 1 thesis.

Separate FACT from INFERENCE.
Example:
- FACT: starting centre-back is unavailable.
- INFERENCE: this may increase defensive volatility against an Under thesis.

Do not label a mixed bundle as `SUPPORTS` if it contains material contradiction.

## Market-specific research questions

### 1X2
1. Is there a material squad-quality imbalance?
2. Is recent performance genuinely strong/weak or are results misleading?
3. What is the strongest football reason for draw/opponent success?

### O/U
1. Are there critical attacking/defensive personnel changes?
2. What is the actual recent scoring/creation/concession profile?
3. What is the strongest game-state path that breaks the total thesis?

### BTTS
1. Does the home side have a credible scoring path?
2. Does the away side have a credible scoring path?
3. Is one side's scoring probability materially stronger/weaker than the Stage 1 thesis assumes?

## Compact Stage 2 output

For each match output only:
- `RESEARCH SUPPORT`: strongest 1-2 reasons;
- `RESEARCH COUNTER`: strongest single contradiction;
- `IMPORTANT ABSENCE`: only when materially relevant;
- `COVERAGE`: HIGH | MEDIUM | LOW;
- `VERDICT`: CONFIRMED | PARTIALLY_CONFIRMED | CONTRADICTED | UNEXPLAINED.

No final BET/WATCH/PASS is issued in Stage 2.

## Relationship to Stage 3

Stage 2 should deliver a compact, structured decision packet rather than a large research dump. The strongest contradiction feeds the active Stage 3 counterevidence severity/penalty framework. Low coverage by itself must not be scored as adverse evidence.
